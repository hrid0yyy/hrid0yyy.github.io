  1. Preprint SSRN 7664934 2026

    Few-shot classification of rare viruses in transmission electron microscopy images using Siamese neural networks

    Rare viruses come with five to twenty-five micrographs and no trained model. I train a Siamese encoder on the fourteen common classes of the public TEM virus dataset, then add the eight rare classes only at test time, as class prototypes. The encoder never sees them.

    14-class test accuracy
    94.8%
    Unseen rare classes, one shot
    37.3% against 30.8% for the re-trained baseline

    At five shots the baseline edges ahead (67.9% against 64.6%). The gain is in the one-shot regime, and the paper reports it that way.

  2. Preprint arXiv 2610.15029 2026

    Is a 9B decision foundation model needed for symptom-to-disease classification? Fine-tuning Clef-Flash against TF‑IDF baselines

    Clef-Flash is a 9B decision foundation model. I fine-tune it with 4-bit QLoRA on the twenty-four-class Symptom2Disease set, then train TF‑IDF linear baselines on exactly the same stratified split. A leave-one-symptom-out ablation on four narratives shows which clause carried each prediction.

    Clef-Flash, after QLoRA
    95.4% from 65.0% zero-shot
    TF‑IDF with a linear SVM
    98.8% at 0.7 ms per record on a laptop CPU

    On short, keyword-rich narratives the 9B model offers no accuracy advantage. That is the finding, not a failed training run.

  1. Dataset DOI 10.17632/33vjwcrpjj.1 2026

    BD-BANKNOTES: a multi-series annotated dataset of Bangladeshi banknotes for object detection

    3,466 field photographs of Bangladeshi paper currency, annotated for detection in YOLO format with bounding boxes and nine denomination classes. It covers both the older circulating series and the 2025–26 architecture series, with fixed 80:10:10 splits.

    Annotated images
    3,466
    Denominations
    9 from 2 to 1,000 Taka, across two series

    Released under CC BY 4.0 with versioned split manifests, so benchmarks on it can be reproduced.
